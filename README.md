# Digital Bahi

*Aapke Business ka Digital Hisab*

Distributor & supplier CRM, billing, inventory, GST and collections for Indian FMCG distributors, wholesalers and suppliers. Web, Windows, Mac, Android and iPhone from one code base (installable app), in English and Hindi. Each firm using it gets its own letterhead: its logo, name, address, mobile, email and GSTIN on every printed document, with Digital Bahi as the app around it.

In the code the name is written `digitalbahi`: the `@digitalbahi/*` packages, the database `digitalbahi` and its roles `digitalbahi_owner` / `digitalbahi_app`. (Until October 2026 the working name was `vyapaar`; a server installed before then keeps its data as described in [HOW-TO-RUN.md](HOW-TO-RUN.md#servers-installed-before-the-rename).)

> **Status:** Phases 1–2 (architecture, UI/UX) are done, the **Phase 3 MVP is built as a multi-tenant SaaS** (a Fastify API over PostgreSQL with web screens for billing, customers, products, suppliers, purchases, payments, inventory, godowns and transfers, sales orders, returns, CSV import, printing and WhatsApp share, reports, GST summary, audit log, users & roles and settings), and **Phase 4 is built**: expenses with GST input credit, GSTR-1 files (JSON for the offline tool, CSV per table) and the GSTR-3B working with month lock and filing record, employees, attendance, leave and payroll with printable salary slips, packing (scan-to-pack, first-expiry-first batches, packing slips) and dispatch (trips, delivery updates, trip sheets), discount / rate-difference credit notes and supplier credit notes, and profit, P&L and product-movement reports. Since then: supplier bill import (Excel, CSV, PDF), PDF reports, **invoice, note and receipt PDFs made on the server** (shared as files from phones), **WhatsApp Business and email** (bills with the PDF and payment reminders from the firm's own number), **Excel reports**, **Backup & Restore** (the whole firm in one password-protected file, restored as a new business), the installable app, and **billing without internet** (device bill series, sync when back online). Also built: **e-invoice and e-way bill files** for upload on the government portals (IRN, acknowledgement and signed QR code entered back and printed; a GSP adapter for direct generation is still to come), **exports and SEZ supplies**, **PF, ESI and professional tax** on salary slips, and returns against reverse-charge purchases. **Phase 5 has started: 5.1, the purchase-history service, is built** — a background worker keeps each customer's buying figures a few seconds after their bills, so the billing screen's suggestions open at once (7 ms instead of 95 ms for a customer with 360 bills in a year), a nightly job rebuilds them and checks them, mines "bought together" rules and checks every cached balance against its ledger, and the billing screen records which suggestions were taken. **5.2, notifications, is built too:** reminder rules (customer and supplier payments due, low stock, expiring stock, orders to confirm, pack and dispatch, leave requests, salary due) checked once a day at each rule's hour, an hourly one for orders waiting too long, notifications as orders move on and leave is asked for and decided, a bell that updates live, and an inbox each person reads on their own, in English and Hindi; and **automatic payment reminders to customers** by WhatsApp, SMS (the firm's DLT-registered gateway) or email, in each customer's language, on a schedule the owner sets, with per-customer opt-out (on the customer's page, or the customer replies STOP); notifications also go to phones as push messages (Firebase Cloud Messaging), in each phone's language, to whoever they are for. **5.3, offline-first, is built as well:** bills made offline that broke a rule by the time they arrived (a price changed, a customer made inactive, a GST month locked, a week late) are held for a manager instead of refused; each device keeps its copy current with only what changed (the pull feed, no catalogue download at each start); customer changes made offline merge field by field; Settings → Devices shows what waits on each device, with a reminder when one keeps work too long. The acceptance test syncs three devices' day offline (300 bills, 100 receipts) at once over a bad connection with no duplicate and every total matching. **5.5, business intelligence, is built:** an *Insights* report with the period's sales, margin, collections, receivables (days sales outstanding) and stock turnover against the previous period or the same dates last year, the biggest movers, ABC contribution of products and customers, and slow and dead stock. The next steps are a pilot with a real distributor and Phase 5.4 (the Android app). See [docs/11-roadmap.md](docs/11-roadmap.md).

### What is implemented

* **`@digitalbahi/core`** — ESC/POS for thermal printers (a receipt drawn as a picture, sent in bands; Hindi prints too) and UPI payment links; GST return formatters (GSTR-1 JSON / offline-tool CSV, GSTR-3B with credit set-off in the rule 88A order, golden-file tests); payroll (payable days, salary slips, advance recovery); exact-decimal money; GST engine with effective-dated, value-conditional rates, intra/inter-state and UTGST, tax-inclusive pricing, cess, invoice-level discount apportionment and round-off; invoice/document totals with rate-wise and HSN summaries; financial-year numbering with GST number-format checks; stock rules (negative-stock policy, FEFO batch picking, moving-average cost, expiry); payment allocation, aging, credit checks and payment behaviour; configurable order workflow; explainable product suggestions (reorder due, frequently bought, bought together, last order) and product search ranking; GSTIN/PAN/IFSC/mobile/pincode/HSN validators; amount in words (English and Hindi); permission catalogue and default roles.
* **`@digitalbahi/i18n`** — typed translation keys, English and Hindi catalogues with parity checks, lakh/crore formatting.
* **`@digitalbahi/db`** — 98 tables covering every module, each column described in the schema and in the [database reference](docs/12-database-reference.md); append-only ledgers, no deletion of posted documents, GST period locks and tenant row-level security enforced by PostgreSQL; reference and per-business seeds.
* **`apps/api`** — one transaction per business action with PostgreSQL row-level security (the API connects as a role that cannot bypass it): OTP sign-up that creates the business with its defaults, password/OTP login, rotating refresh tokens with theft detection, several businesses per user, members and roles with anti-escalation rules; masters with GSTIN checks and duplicate hints; invoice posting (gap-free numbers under concurrency, rate lists, dated GST rules, server-verified totals, stock and party ledgers, GST register, payment at sale, idempotency keys), cancellation, purchase bills (batches, landed and moving-average cost, reverse charge, ITC), stock ledger and adjustments, receipts and supplier payments (allocation, advances, cheque bounce), statements, dashboard, reports with CSV export, GST summary, search, audit log; expenses, GST return files and month locking, employees / attendance / leave / payroll, packing and dispatch, value-only credit notes, profit / P&L / product reports. Supplier and employee bank accounts, PAN and Aadhaar are encrypted at rest. Background work runs in the API process: an outbox worker keeps each customer's purchase history (what they buy and how often, payment behaviour) a few seconds after their bills, payments and notes; a nightly job rebuilds it, checks it for drift, mines "bought together" rules and checks every cached balance and stock quantity against its ledger; automatic backups go out on schedule. Reorder lists per customer and for a beat, and feedback on which suggestions were taken (`/suggestions/feedback`). Beats: a salesperson's routes for the day with the shops in calling order, what each is due to buy again and how the day is going, visits with no order and why, and each salesperson's day against the plan.
* **`apps/web`** — sign-up / sign-in (password or OTP) and business picker; app shell (desktop sidebar, phone bottom navigation), login, dashboard, **create invoice** (desktop and phone flows, live GST, smart suggestions with reasons, keyboard shortcuts, barcode entry, credit warnings), printable tax invoice in English or Hindi, customers, customer profile (ledger, reorder list), products, settings (language, theme, devices for offline billing), **offline billing** (opens without a network, bills numbered by the device and synced later), global search (Ctrl+K), design-system gallery, and a page for every planned module showing its tables and endpoints.

## Getting started

**Step-by-step instructions for everyone** (running it on a computer, installing the desktop app on Windows / Mac / Linux and the mobile app on Android / iPhone, putting it on a server with your own web address, backups and troubleshooting) are in **[HOW-TO-RUN.md](HOW-TO-RUN.md)**. The short version for developers:

**Just running it on one computer?** On Windows run `DigitalBahi-Setup-<version>.exe` (from a release, or `node installer/build-bundle.mjs --target win32-x64 --exe`); on macOS and Linux run `installer/unix/digitalbahi.sh`. Either installs what is missing (Node.js, PostgreSQL), sets up a database of its own and opens the app at http://localhost:8710: see [the installer](HOW-TO-RUN.md#the-quick-way-the-installer). The API then also serves the built web app (`WEB_DIR`), and `installer/smoke-test.mjs` checks a running copy (sign-up, a bill and its PDF).

Requirements: Node.js 22+, pnpm 10, PostgreSQL 16+.

```bash
pnpm install
pnpm lint        # ESLint: hooks rules, unused code, stray escapes
pnpm typecheck
# unit tests, plus database and API integration tests (each test file creates and drops its own database)
DATABASE_URL=postgres://postgres:postgres@localhost:5432/postgres pnpm test
pnpm build
```

### Run it locally

```bash
createdb digitalbahi_dev                                  # as the database owner
# migrations, the restricted application role (digitalbahi_app) and reference data
MIGRATION_DATABASE_URL=postgres://postgres:postgres@localhost:5432/digitalbahi_dev APP_DB_PASSWORD=devpass pnpm db:deploy
# API on :3000 (OpenAPI at http://localhost:3000/api/docs); OTP codes are shown in the sign-up screen
DATABASE_URL=postgres://digitalbahi_app:devpass@localhost:5432/digitalbahi_dev OTP_DEV_ECHO=true pnpm dev:api
pnpm dev:web                                          # http://localhost:5173, proxies /api to the API
```

To bring an existing business in, use **Products → Import** and **Customers → Import**: download the template or upload the Excel file (.xlsx), a CSV, or a PDF made by other billing software (a company's price list, for example; common header names are recognised); every row is checked first and nothing is saved until the whole file is right.

To enter a supplier's bill from the file they send, use **Purchases → Import bill**: upload their Excel or CSV bill, or the PDF their billing software printed (exports from Marg, Tally, Busy or a company DMS work: title rows above the header, bill number only on the first line, split CGST / SGST, scheme quantity, PTR, batch and expiry are all understood; in a PDF the bill number, date and the supplier's GSTIN are read from above the table, item names that wrap onto more lines are joined, whether printed under the first line or above and below the numbers as big companies' invoices do, headings over two or three lines are understood, GST written as "CGST@6 + SGST@6" or "IGST@18" is read as the rate, and a table that runs on to the next page without its heading is followed to the totals). The purchase keeps the GST the supplier charged, so bills from before a rate change (such as the 22 Sep 2025 one) are entered as printed. A scanned or photographed PDF has no text to read and gets a message asking for the Excel or the software's PDF instead. The supplier is found by GSTIN or name, and each item by barcode, code or name; the rest you pick from close matches or add as new products. The bill then opens in purchase entry for checking, and the matches you keep are remembered for that supplier, so their next bill matches on its own. A bill already entered is flagged.

Every report (sales, collections, purchases, outstanding, expenses, profit, product movement) downloads as **PDF**, **Excel (.xlsx)** or **CSV**. The Excel workbook keeps numbers and dates as such (sums and filters work), with the title, firm and period on top, a frozen heading row and the totals. The PDF carries the firm's letterhead (logo, name, address, contact, GSTIN), the period, the table with its totals and page numbers, and prints Hindi names correctly.

Demo mode without a server: `VITE_DATA_SOURCE=mock pnpm dev:web`, or `pnpm --filter @digitalbahi/web build:preview` for a single shareable HTML file. In demo mode: **New Invoice** (Ctrl+I) → type `ABC` and press Enter → the suggestions explain themselves ("Usually every 7 days · last bought 6 days ago") → Alt+1 adds the first → F2 and type `namak` → Ctrl+Enter saves. Switch **EN / हिं** in the top bar at any time.

### Deploy (one server)

```bash
cp .env.example .env                  # fill in passwords, domain and the OTP gateway URL
pnpm --filter @digitalbahi/api keys       # prints JWT_PRIVATE_KEY, JWT_PUBLIC_KEY, DATA_ENCRYPTION_KEY for .env
docker compose up -d --build          # PostgreSQL, release job (migrations), API, Caddy with TLS
```

The API refuses to start in production without signing keys, the data-encryption key and an OTP gateway (`OTP_WEBHOOK_URL`, which receives `{mobile, code, purpose}` and sends the SMS/WhatsApp). Back up `DATA_ENCRYPTION_KEY`: data encrypted with a lost key cannot be recovered.

## Notes for reviewers

* GST rates, thresholds and product data in seeds and samples are **illustrative**; the system stores them as dated configuration to be verified against current notifications before go-live.
* Keyboard shortcuts: Ctrl+K (search) and Ctrl+I (invoice) are as requested; purchase and collection use **Alt+P / Alt+C** because Ctrl+P and Ctrl+C are the browser's Print and Copy. On the bill being made, Ctrl+S saves it and Ctrl+P saves and prints it (there the browser's print of the screen is of no use).
